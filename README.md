Detailed guide and project requirements for the Order Channel Performance and Market Share Analytics for SkyCity Auckland Restaurants &amp; Bars analysis.
 Order Channel Performance and Market Share 
Analytics for SkyCity Auckland Restaurants & Bars 
Author: ANANT SINGH
Abstract 
This paper analyzes multi-channel ordering systems in SkyCity Auckland's restaurant ecosystem. 
The study focuses on evaluating order distribution across in-store and delivery channels, identifying 
market share trends, and detecting dependency risks. The findings highlight the increasing 
dominance of third-party aggregators and emphasize the need for diversified channel strategies to 
ensure long-term sustainability. 
I. Introduction 
The rapid expansion of food delivery platforms such as Uber Eats and DoorDash has significantly 
transformed the hospitality industry. Restaurants now operate through multiple parallel channels, 
including in-store dining, aggregator platforms, and self-managed delivery. This multi-channel 
environment presents both opportunities and risks. Understanding channel performance is 
essential for optimizing revenue, customer reach, and operational efficiency. 
II. Methodology 
The dataset used in this study contains restaurant-level data, including order counts, revenue, 
costs, and channel share percentages. Data validation was performed to ensure consistency in 
order counts and percentage distributions. Aggregation techniques were applied to compute 
channel-wise market share. Further segmentation analysis was conducted based on subregions, 
cuisine types, and restaurant categories. Dependency risk was evaluated using aggregator share 
thresholds, and diversification was measured using a probabilistic scoring model. 
III. Results and Analysis 
The analysis reveals that delivery platforms, particularly Uber Eats and DoorDash, contribute a 
significant portion of total orders. Central Auckland exhibits a balanced mix of in-store and delivery 
channels, whereas suburban regions show a higher reliance on aggregators. Cuisine-based 
analysis indicates that fast-food categories such as burgers and pizza are heavily dependent on 
delivery platforms, while cafes rely more on in-store orders. A substantial number of restaurants 
demonstrate over 70 percent dependency on aggregators, indicating high operational risk. 
IV. Discussion 
The findings suggest that while aggregator platforms enhance market reach, excessive reliance 
increases commission costs and reduces profitability. Restaurants with a diversified channel mix 
show greater resilience and improved financial performance. Self-delivery models provide an 
opportunity to reduce dependency and improve margin control. Strategic channel optimization is 
therefore essential for sustainable growth. 
V. Conclusion 
This study highlights the importance of channel performance analytics in modern restaurant 
operations. By leveraging data-driven insights, restaurants can reduce risk, improve profitability, 
and make informed strategic decisions. Future research can incorporate predictive modeling and 
real-time analytics to further enhance decision-making processes.
